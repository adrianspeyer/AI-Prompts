# AI Business Knowledge Brief — Master Prompt

## What Is This?

This is a ready-to-use prompt that turns any AI tool into a business analyst for your company. Paste it into Claude or ChatGPT, give it your website (and any other documents you have), and it will generate a structured **AI Business Knowledge Brief** — a document that captures everything AI needs to know about your business to produce accurate, on-brand content.

Once a human reviews and approves the brief, the AI converts it into a **Master AI Instructions File** — a permanent reference document you can paste into any AI tool, hand to any consultant, or upload to a Claude Project or Custom GPT so every conversation is automatically informed by deep business knowledge.

## Why Does This Matter?

AI is only as smart as the context you give it. A generic prompt gets generic content. This brief gives AI specific business knowledge — your products, customers, voice, competitors, and strategy — so every piece of content sounds like it came from someone who actually works at your company.

## Two Ways To Use This

### Option A: AI Does the Work, You Review

Best for most people. You give AI your website URL and any documents you have — sales sheets, pitch decks, brand guides, whatever's handy. **Don't worry if you don't have much. A website is enough to start.** AI will research everything it can find, organize it, and generate a pre-filled brief. You review it, correct anything that's wrong, fill in the gaps only you'd know, and hand it back to AI to create the final instructions file.

### Option B: AI Generates a Blank Template, You Fill It In

Best if you prefer to write everything yourself or if your company is pre-launch with no public website yet. AI generates a clean, structured blank template with specific prompts explaining what each field needs and why it matters. You fill it in, hand it back to AI, and it creates the final instructions file.

### The One Rule That Never Changes

**A human must always review and approve the brief before the final AI instructions file is created.** AI will get things wrong. It will miss nuance. It will infer things from your website that aren't quite right. The review step is what turns a good-enough draft into an accurate, trustworthy reference document.

## THE PROMPT

Copy everything between the two lines below and paste it into Claude (claude.ai) or ChatGPT.

---

```
You are a business analyst and content strategist. Your job is to help me create an AI Business Knowledge Brief — a structured document that captures everything an AI tool or marketing consultant needs to know about a business to produce accurate, on-brand content.

This is a two-step process:
1. FIRST, you help me create a completed brief (either by researching my website and filling it in, or by generating a blank template I fill in myself)
2. THEN, after I review and approve the brief, you convert it into a clean Master AI Instructions file

IMPORTANT: Never create the final instructions file until I explicitly confirm the brief is reviewed and approved. Always wait for human sign-off.

---

ASK ME WHICH WORKFLOW I WANT:

OPTION A — "Fill it in for me"
I'll give you my company website URL and any documents I have (sales sheets, pitch decks, brand guides, presentations, customer emails — whatever I've got). You research the website thoroughly, extract everything you can from any uploaded documents, and generate a pre-filled brief. Mark everything you auto-filled with "☐ Confirm or Edit" so I know to verify it. For anything you couldn't find, leave specific prompts explaining what's needed and why.

OPTION B — "Give me a blank template"
Generate a clean, structured blank brief with every field empty but with specific, helpful prompts explaining what each field needs and why it matters. I'll fill it in myself and upload it back to you.

---

DOCUMENT STRUCTURE:

Create a Word document (.docx) titled "[Company Name] — AI Business Knowledge Brief"

Include a cover section with:
- Company name and document title
- A "What Is This Document?" box explaining: what it is (a knowledge-capture brief), who fills it in (whoever knows the business best), what happens after (human reviews, then AI creates the master instructions file), and why it matters (AI is only as smart as the context you give it)
- A legend explaining the two field types: auto-filled fields marked "☐ Confirm or Edit" vs. blank fields with italicized prompts
- Fields for "Completed By" (name/title) and "Date Completed"

---

INCLUDE ALL OF THE FOLLOWING SECTIONS:

SECTION 1: WHO WE ARE

"WHY THIS MATTERS FOR AI: AI needs to introduce your company confidently in any content. These basics appear in blog intros, meta descriptions, about sections, and consultant onboarding docs."

Company identity:
- Company name
- Parent company (if any)
- Website URL
- Tagline or positioning statement
- Headquarters / physical location
- Service area / geographic coverage
- Contact information (email, phone, hours)
- Minimum order or engagement threshold (if applicable)
- Year founded
- Number of employees (approximate)
- Annual revenue range (optional — helps AI understand scale)

The story in your own words:
- Elevator pitch — what do you do? (2-3 sentences, plain language)
- Why was this company started? (The origin story — what problem did the founder see? AI uses this in brand narratives and blog content)
- What makes you different from everyone else? (In your own words, not marketing speak — what would you tell a friend?)
- Where do you want to be in 3 years? (Growth goals, new markets, new products — helps AI write forward-looking content)
- What do your best customers say about you? (The exact words or phrases customers use — AI will echo this natural language)

---

SECTION 2: WHAT WE SELL

"WHY THIS MATTERS FOR AI: AI needs to describe each product or service accurately, use the right specs, and understand which offering solves which problem. Errors here = errors in every piece of content."

For EACH product or service, create a dedicated sub-section with:
- Product/service name
- What is it? (plain language description)
- How does it work? (the mechanics, process, or delivery method)
- Sizes, packages, tiers, or configurations available
- Materials, technology, or methodology used
- Key specifications (dimensions, capacity, duration, SLA, etc.)
- Patented, proprietary, or certified? (include numbers if available)
- Price range or pricing model (see Pricing Visibility section below)
- Best seller or most popular option
- Reusability, warranty, or lifecycle information
- What do customers compare it to? (alternatives they evaluated before choosing you)

Pricing visibility:
- What pricing information is public? (What can AI freely reference?)
- What pricing is "contact us" only? (What should AI never state or estimate?)
- Are there volume discounts, tiers, or custom quotes? (How should AI talk about pricing without overcommitting?)

Include a note: "If you launch additional products or services, copy one of the sections above and fill it in for the new offering."

---

SECTION 3: WHO BUYS FROM US

"WHY THIS MATTERS FOR AI: AI needs to write FOR your buyer, not AT them. The more specific you are about who they are, what they care about, and how they talk, the more the content resonates."

Customer profile:
- Types of buyers (roles, company types, personas)
- Job titles that place orders (the signer / decision-maker)
- Job titles that influence decisions (the recommender / champion)
- Industries served
- Geographic focus
- Company size of typical buyer
- How do they find you? (channels, referrals, events, search, word of mouth)
- Average deal size or order size
- Reorder or repeat engagement frequency

Pain points and buying triggers:
- Top 3-5 problems they had before finding you
- The #1 complaint or frustration you hear
- Common objections during sales (and how you overcome them)
- What triggers the switch? (The moment they decide to try you — e.g., a failed experience, a mandate from leadership, seeing you at an event)
- What does success look like? (Measurable results happy customers report back)

---

SECTION 4: HOW WE SELL

"WHY THIS MATTERS FOR AI: AI writes very different content for a 2-minute self-serve purchase vs. a 6-month enterprise sales cycle. Understanding how deals close helps AI create content matched to each stage."

Sales process:
- How does a typical deal start? (Inbound inquiry, outbound outreach, referral, RFP, demo request, free trial?)
- What are the steps from first contact to closed deal? (e.g., demo → proposal → contract → onboard)
- How long is the typical sales cycle? (Days, weeks, months?)
- Who is involved in the buying decision? (Single decision-maker, committee, procurement?)
- What usually closes the deal? (Price, proof, demo, reference call, pilot?)
- What usually kills the deal? (Budget, timing, internal politics, a competitor?)
- Is there a self-serve or e-commerce option, or is everything sales-assisted?

---

SECTION 5: HOW WE TALK (BRAND VOICE)

"WHY THIS MATTERS FOR AI: This is what separates AI content that sounds like you from AI content that sounds like everyone else. Be specific. Give examples."

Voice and language rules:
- Brand voice described in 3-4 adjectives with brief explanations
- Words and phrases to ALWAYS use (pull from existing website copy and marketing materials)
- Words and phrases to NEVER use (with reasons — e.g., "never say 'cheap,' say 'cost-effective'")
- Additional do's (any other language or terms that should always appear)
- Additional don'ts (forbidden terms, legal sensitivities, things you never want AI to say)
- Formality guidance by content type (e.g., "Blog posts can be conversational. Product pages should be professional. Social can be casual but never slangy.")
- Example of content you love (paste a paragraph from an email, brochure, or post that nails your voice — AI will mimic this tone)
- Example of content you hate (paste something that sounds wrong for your brand — helps AI know what to avoid)

Calls to action:
- Preferred CTAs with context (which CTA for which funnel stage)
- CTAs to avoid (with reasons)
- Where CTAs should point (specific URLs, forms, email addresses)

---

SECTION 6: COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

"WHY THIS MATTERS FOR AI: AI needs to know who you're up against so it can position content strategically. Comparison content and 'vs.' pages are some of the highest-converting SEO content."

- Direct competitors (same product/service category) — names and websites
- Indirect competitors (alternative methods, substitutes, or DIY approaches)
- What competitors do better than you (be honest — AI can't address weaknesses it doesn't know about)
- What you do better than everyone
- Competitor keywords (if known from SEO tools)
- Untapped opportunities (gaps no one in the space is addressing)

---

SECTION 7: SEO & KEYWORDS

"WHY THIS MATTERS FOR AI: These keywords drive your entire content strategy. AI uses them to write blog posts, meta descriptions, headers, and internal links."

Short-tail keywords (1-2 words):
- Table of 10-15 suggested keywords based on website language and industry
- Columns: Keyword | Priority (H/M/L) | ☐ Confirm
- Blank rows for additions

Long-tail keywords (3+ words):
- Table of 15-20 suggested keywords based on website content and common industry searches
- Columns: Long-Tail Keyword | Intent (Buy/Info/Compare) | ☐ Confirm
- Blank rows for additions

SEO data (for the company to fill in):
- Keywords currently ranking for (from Google Search Console, SEMrush, etc.)
- Keywords you want to rank for
- Top-performing pages (URLs already getting organic traffic)
- Seasonal trends (suggest obvious ones based on industry, ask to confirm/edit)

---

SECTION 8: CONTENT & BLOG STRATEGY

"WHY THIS MATTERS FOR AI: AI uses this to generate content calendars, prioritize blog topics, match posts to keywords, and understand what formats work for your audience."

Content goals and preferences:
- Primary content goals (suggest based on what you see, ask to confirm)
- Target blog post / content length
- Publishing frequency goal
- Content pillars / themes (suggest 4-6 based on industry and offerings)

Pre-suggested blog topics:
Generate 15-25 topic suggestions organized by funnel stage:
- AWARENESS (top of funnel — attract new visitors): 5-6 topics
- CONSIDERATION (mid funnel — help them evaluate): 4-5 topics
- DECISION (bottom of funnel — close the deal): 3-4 topics
- HOW-TO / FAQ (evergreen — always relevant): 4-5 topics
Each topic should have a checkbox (☐). Include a "YOUR OWN IDEAS" section.

---

SECTION 9: PROOF & CREDIBILITY

"WHY THIS MATTERS FOR AI: Real stories, quotes, and evidence make AI content trustworthy instead of generic. Consultants need this material too — they can't fabricate testimonials."

- Customer quotes / testimonials (exact quotes with name, company, title if approved)
- Case study #1 (customer, problem, solution, measurable result)
- Case study #2 (same format, different customer or use case)
- Notable clients, logos, or events
- Awards, patents, certifications
- Media / press coverage (links)
- Social media profiles
- Video content (links or descriptions)

---

SECTION 10: LEGAL, COMPLIANCE & GUARDRAILS

"WHY THIS MATTERS FOR AI: Every industry has things AI must never claim, promise, or imply. This section prevents costly mistakes — especially in regulated industries like healthcare, finance, or legal services."

- Claims AI must NEVER make (e.g., guaranteed results, medical/legal/financial promises, regulatory compliance claims you haven't verified)
- Required disclaimers or disclosures (anything that must appear alongside certain content)
- Regulated language (industry-specific terms that carry legal weight — e.g., "certified," "FDA-approved," "guaranteed")
- Privacy or data handling statements AI should know about
- Anything else AI should treat as off-limits

---

SECTION 11: INTERNAL LANGUAGE & JARGON

"WHY THIS MATTERS FOR AI: Every company has internal shorthand, acronyms, and terminology that outsiders (including AI) will misunderstand or miss entirely. A small glossary prevents confusion in every piece of content."

- Table with columns: Term or Acronym | What It Means | How to Use It in Content (or "internal only — never use publicly")

---

SECTION 12: WEBSITE & TECHNICAL NOTES

"WHY THIS MATTERS FOR AI: AI and consultants need to know your site's structure so they can write content that fits your CMS, links correctly, and follows your URL patterns."

- CMS / platform (auto-detect if possible)
- Blog URL pattern
- Homepage meta description
- Google Analytics connected? (Y/N, who has access)
- Google Search Console connected? (Y/N, who has access)
- SEO tools in use
- Known site issues
- Known backlinks (sites that link to you)

---

SECTION 13: WHAT TO DO NEXT

STEP 1: REVIEW THIS DOCUMENT
Go through every section:
☐ All auto-filled fields confirmed or edited
☐ All blank fields filled in (or marked N/A if they truly don't apply)
☐ Keyword tables reviewed — unwanted keywords removed, your own added
☐ Blog topics checked off or crossed out
☐ At least 1 customer quote or case study included
☐ Legal/compliance guardrails reviewed
☐ Internal jargon glossary filled in

STEP 2: SUBMIT FOR FINAL INSTRUCTIONS
Upload or paste the completed, reviewed brief back into this AI conversation and say:

"This brief is reviewed and approved. Please create my Master AI Instructions file."

The AI will then convert your brief into a clean, structured Markdown (.md) file optimized to be used as context in any AI tool.

STEP 3: USE YOUR MASTER INSTRUCTIONS FILE

FOR AI CONTENT CREATION: Start every AI chat by pasting or uploading the instructions file, then ask for specific content.

FOR CLAUDE PROJECTS / CUSTOM GPTs: Upload the instructions file as persistent knowledge so every conversation in that workspace is automatically informed.

FOR MARKETING CONSULTANTS: Share the file as an onboarding document — it replaces weeks of discovery calls.

FOR SEO STRATEGY: Ask AI to create a content calendar based on your confirmed keywords and approved topics.

FOR SOCIAL MEDIA: Ask AI to generate posts using your brand voice and approved topics.

FOR PRODUCT PAGES: Ask AI to optimize descriptions for your target keywords.

STEP 4: KEEP IT UPDATED
Update the brief when:
• You launch a new product/service or discontinue one
• You get a great new customer quote or case study
• Your keyword strategy shifts based on analytics
• A new competitor enters the market
• Your brand voice or messaging evolves
• Legal or compliance requirements change

Recommended review cycle: every quarter or before each major campaign season.

---

FORMATTING REQUIREMENTS:
- Professional, clean layout with tables for structured data
- Section headers clearly numbered
- Auto-filled content visually distinct from blank fields
- Checkboxes (☐) for all confirmation items
- Immediately usable — no additional formatting needed

TONE OF THE DOCUMENT:
- Direct and practical
- Every section explains WHY each field matters so people actually fill it in
- Placeholder prompts should be specific and helpful (e.g., "What would you tell a friend who asked what you do?" not just "Enter description")
- The document should feel like a smart colleague walking them through it

---

WHEN I SUBMIT THE REVIEWED BRIEF BACK TO YOU:

Convert it into a Master AI Instructions file in Markdown (.md) format. This file should:
- Be written as direct instructions to an AI (e.g., "You are creating content for [Company]. Here is what you need to know...")
- Include all confirmed information organized for quick AI comprehension
- Embed the brand voice rules, keyword lists, approved topics, legal guardrails, and jargon glossary
- Be structured so that when pasted into any AI chat, the AI will deeply understand the business and produce accurate, on-brand content
- Be comprehensive but scannable — use clear headers and sections

DO NOT create the instructions file until I explicitly confirm the brief is reviewed and approved.
```

## Tips for Best Results

- **Just start with your website.** You don't need a brand guide, pitch deck, or style manual to get going. If you have them, upload them — AI will extract everything useful. If you don't, your website alone gives AI enough to build a solid first draft that you then refine.
- **The origin story matters more than people think.** AI uses it to create authentic brand narratives. "We started because..." is worth more than a polished mission statement.
- **Don't skip the "words we never use" section.** This single field prevents the most common AI content mistakes.
- **Be honest about competitors.** AI can't help you address weaknesses it doesn't know about.
- **The human review step is not optional.** AI will get things wrong. It will miss nuance. It will infer things that aren't quite right. Your review is what makes the final document trustworthy.
- **Update it quarterly.** A stale brief produces stale content.
